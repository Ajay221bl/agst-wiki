Construct validity asks whether your measurements actually capture the idea you care about. That idea is called the construct.

Suppose you want to know how hungry a dog is, so you count how fast its tail wags. Wagging is easy to count, but is it hunger? It might be excitement, or that the owner just walked in. You've measured something precisely, but maybe not the thing you wanted. That gap, between the idea and the number standing in for it, is what this note is about.

The question here is humbler than it sounds: are you measuring what you think you're measuring? A study measures variables ([[Variable and Observation]]). Some, like body weight, can be put on a scale. Others are ideas rather than things: "psychological distress" can't be weighed. So it is measured indirectly, for example by a survey score built from answers to questions. Construct validity asks whether that score really tracks the idea. If the questions track distress, the measure has good construct validity. If they track something else, such as mood that morning or reading level, it doesn't.

This matters because a perfect analysis of the wrong quantity answers the wrong question. It matters most for surveys and questionnaires, which are common in education and social science. Poorly worded questions are one common cause of trouble ([[Sampling Bias]]), and better measures help.

For example, a question that asks two things at once, such as "Do you like animals or go to the movies?", can't measure either attitude, so anything built on it is muddled.

Measurement is where abstract ideas meet numbers, and a lot can be lost in that translation. Reliable doesn't mean valid. A bathroom scale that always reads 2 kg heavy is reliable, because it is consistent, but it isn't valid.

Try it yourself. A study measures "intelligence" by how fast people finish a written test in English. For a group of people still learning English, what might the score really be measuring? Partly English reading speed, not intelligence. The measure lacks construct validity for that group.
