This version of the wiki is set up for revising for an oral exam on experimental statistics, covering everything up to and including one-way ANOVA. Oral exam questions are about ideas: explain a concept in words, compare two ideas, or say why a method is used. Formulas and R code aren't asked, so the notes on them are background only.

## Start here
- **[[Oral Exam Practice]]**: exam-style questions, each with a short spoken answer and links to the notes behind it. Say each answer out loud before you read it.
- **[[MOC - Why We Do It This Way]]**: the "why" notes. Most oral questions are "why" questions, and these notes answer them in full.

## A study order for the last few days
Work through the practice questions one section at a time. For each section, read the linked "why" notes first, then the idea notes they point to.
1. **Describing data and study design**: practice questions 1 to 6, with [[MOC - Describing Data]] and [[MOC - Study Design and Sampling]].
2. **Probability and inference**: questions 7 to 16, with [[MOC - Probability]] and [[MOC - Inference Foundations]]. This is the biggest section, and the ideas in it come back in every test.
3. **t-tests**: questions 17 to 20, with [[MOC - t-Tests]].
4. **One-way ANOVA**: questions 21 to 29, with [[MOC - ANOVA]]. It ties everything together, so leave time for it.
5. **Last pass**: answer a random handful of questions out loud without looking, and revisit the ones that felt shaky.
