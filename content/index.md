These notes explain the statistics used to plan and analyse experiments: probability, describing data, study design, drawing conclusions from samples, t-tests and one-way ANOVA. Every example is worked in R. Each note explains one idea from the ground up. It says what the idea is, why it is done this way, and what goes wrong if it isn't.

## How the notes fit together
- **Idea notes** explain one idea each. They start from zero, build step by step on simpler notes, and come with a figure and a small worked example.
- **Why notes** each answer one "why" question by combining several idea notes.
- **Maps** group the notes by topic and give a reading order.

- **Rusty on maths symbols?** Start with [[MOC - Basics from Zero]]. It explains $\sum$, squares, Greek letters and `2.2e-16` from scratch.
- **Want an order to follow?** Use the [[Learning Path]].

  - [[MOC - Probability]]: events, the rules for combining them, and diagnostic tests
  - [[MOC - Describing Data]]: centre, spread, shape and plots

  - [[MOC - Inference Foundations]]: how a sample tells you about a population, with confidence intervals and tests
  - [[MOC - t-Tests]]: comparing one or two means, and effect size
  - [[MOC - ANOVA]]: comparing three or more means, and the follow-up tests
  - [[MOC - R for Statistics]]: every R function used
- **Want the reasons behind each rule?** Read [[MOC - Why We Do It This Way]].
- **Choosing a test?** Use the [[Test Choice Guide]] and the [[Formula Sheet]].
- **Want to browse everything?** See [[All Notes]] and the [[References]].
