Before any test, I need to know what kind of data I have, where it sits, how spread out it is and what shape it takes.

*Reading:* [[References|Vu and Harrington 2020, Ch. 1]].

## 1. What kind of data
- [[Types of Data]]: categorical vs quantitative, and why it decides everything else
- [[Descriptive vs Inferential Statistics]]: describing my sample vs speaking about the population
- [[Missing Data]]: decide, document, report
- [[Likert Scale]]: ordered survey answers, and why they are ordinal

## 2. Centre
- [[Mean]]: the balance point, unbiased, pulled by tails
- [[Median]]: the middle value, robust to skew
- [[Mode]]: the only centre for nominal data

## 3. Spread
- [[Variability]]: why values differ, and why statistics exists
- [[Percentiles and Quantiles]]: the value below which a given share of the data falls
- [[Range and IQR]]: rank-based spreads that go with the median
- [[Deviations from the Mean]]: each value's distance from the mean, and why they sum to zero
- [[Variance]]: average squared distance from the mean
- [[Standard Deviation]]: the variance back in original units
- [[Coefficient of Variation]]: spread relative to the mean
- [[Effect of Shifting Data]]: adding a constant moves the centre, not the spread

## 4. Shape and unusual values
- [[Shape of a Distribution]]: modality, skew, kurtosis
- [[Tails of a Distribution]]: the rare far ends, where skew shows and p-values live
- [[Outliers]]: IQR fences and z beyond ±2

## 5. Pictures
- [[Exploratory Data Analysis]]: check, describe and plot before testing
- [[Histogram]]: one quantitative variable
- [[Box Plot and Five-Number Summary]]: centre, spread and outliers in one picture
- [[Plots for Categorical Data]]: bars, not pies
- [[Plots for Two Variables]]: match the plot to the two variable types
- [[Correlation]]: r, how tightly two quantitative variables follow a line
