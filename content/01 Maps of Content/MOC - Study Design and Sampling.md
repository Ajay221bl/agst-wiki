> The most important idea here is [[Why random assignment and random sampling protect different things]]. Most questions about validity come back to it.

What a study can conclude is decided before any data exist.

*Reading:* [[References|Kirk 2013, Ch. 1]], [[References|Vu and Harrington 2020, Ch. 1]] §1.3.

## 1. Asking the question
- [[Population and Sample]]: who you want to know about versus who you actually measure
- [[Parameter vs Statistic]]: a number that describes the population versus a number worked out from the sample
- [[PICO Question]]: turning a scientific question into a statistical one
- [[Independent and Dependent Variables]]: what you change and what you measure
- [[Nuisance Variables and Confounding]]: everything else that also moves the outcome
- [[Association vs Causation]]: two things going together versus one causing the other

## 2. Kinds of study
- [[Experiment]]: the researcher changes something and assigns units to groups at random
- [[Treatment and Control Groups]]: the comparison that shows what would have happened anyway
- [[Balanced Design]]: equal group sizes, which give more power and safer tests
- [[Quasi-Experiment]]: like an experiment, but one of those two parts is missing
- [[Randomized Controlled Trial]]: the gold standard of study design
- [[Observational Study Designs]]: cohort, case-control and cross-sectional studies, where the researcher only watches
- [[Blinding and Placebo]]: keeping people's beliefs out of the result

## 3. Two kinds of randomness
- [[Random Assignment]]: balances the groups, which lets you claim cause and effect
- [[Random Sampling]]: makes the sample represent the population, which lets you generalize
- [[Sampling Methods]]: simple random, stratified, cluster and systematic sampling, plus the non-random kinds
- [[Sampling Bias]]: a lean in the sample that a bigger sample cannot fix

## 4. Four kinds of validity
- [[Validity]]: an overview, and how validity differs from reliability
- [[Internal Validity]]: did the independent variable really cause the change?
- [[External Validity]]: how far do the results carry over to other people and settings?
- [[Statistical Conclusion Validity]]: are the statistics sound?
- [[Construct Validity]]: are you measuring what you think you are measuring?
