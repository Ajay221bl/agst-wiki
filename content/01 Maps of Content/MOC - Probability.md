Probability is the language a p-value is written in.

*Reading:* [[References|Vu and Harrington 2020, Ch. 2]].

## 1. The building blocks
- [[Sample Space and Events]]: every possible outcome, and the ones I care about
- [[Classical vs Relative Frequency Probability]]: counting on paper vs counting what happened
- [[Law of Large Numbers]]: why relative frequencies settle down
- [[Random Variable]]: a number decided by chance, and its distribution
- [[Probability Distribution]]: every possible value and its chance, as bars or as area under a curve
- [[Expected Value]]: the long-run average of a random variable

## 2. Combining events
- [[Complementary Events]]: $1 - P(A)$, the "at least one" shortcut
- [[Disjoint Events]]: can't happen together
- [[Independent Events]]: one doesn't change the other (not the same as disjoint)
- [[Addition Rule]]: "or", minus the overlap
- [[Multiplication Rule]]: "and", with or without replacement
- [[Sampling With and Without Replacement]]: put it back (independent) or keep it out (dependent)
- [[Venn Diagram]]: events as circles, probabilities as areas

## 3. Conditioning
- [[Contingency Table]]: the table every conditional question is read from
- [[Conditional Probability]]: shrink the sample space to the condition
- [[Bayes' Theorem]]: flipping the condition
- [[Odds and Odds Ratio]]: happens over doesn't happen, and comparing two groups

## 4. Diagnostic testing
- [[Sensitivity and Specificity]]: properties of the test itself
- [[Predictive Values and Prevalence]]: what a result means for me, which depends on how common the condition is

## 5. The bridge to inference
- [[Rare Event Rule]]: very unlikely under an assumption means the assumption is probably wrong
